A Brief History

Long before foreign ships arrived, the land around the Davao Gulf was home to indigenous peoples such as the Bagobo, Mandaya, Mansaka and Matigsalug. They farmed the river valleys, lived in the mountains and traded with their neighbors. Different groups tell different stories about where the name "Davao" comes from.

In the mid-1800s a Spanish expedition led by Jose Cruz de Uyanguren came to the gulf and set up a settlement called Nueva Vergara. Local leaders, including the Bagobo chieftain Datu Bago, resisted, and Spanish control stayed limited. In the early 1900s, during the American period, large abaca (Manila hemp) plantations began to grow. Workers and settlers came from other parts of the Philippines and from Japan, and Davao grew quickly. It became a chartered city in 1937.

After the Second World War, more families moved to Mindanao from the Visayas and Luzon, which made the region the mix of cultures it is today. Cebuano is widely spoken, and many people also speak Tagalog and English. Indigenous traditions are still alive in the region's festivals, weaving, music and food.

Cultural Highlights

August

Kadayawan Festival

Davao City. A thanksgiving for the harvest and for the many cultures of the region, with a flower parade and street dancing.

March

Araw ng Dabaw

Davao City. The city's founding anniversary, with parades, programs and community events.

Davao Oriental

Pasidungog Festival

A celebration that honors the province's people and culture.

Davao de Oro

Bulawan Festival

A festival named after the local word for gold, which the province is known for.
